It’s a challenging style to pin down, and many in the industry question whether “white rum” is even a useful catchall for spirits that are distilled around the world using an array of different methods, and can be unaged, or aged and then filtered. Yet it’s a designation that continues to be widely used, despite the collection of rums it describes becoming ever more diverse and characterful.

“Consumers have historically been led to believe colour means age and age equals quality, when neither is really accurate in the rum category,” says Andy Huntley, national brand ambassador for Diplomático. “White describes how the liquid looks, but it doesn’t tell you how it was made or how much character it has.”
