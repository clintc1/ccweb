ve just landed in Goa and no, I’m not here for a yoga retreat, not joining the ranks of harem-pants-wearing hippies headed east in search of spirituality on a picturesque beach. I seek a different spirit – whisky – and, as it turns out, I’m in the right place. 

I’m in excellent company too. Indian drinkers consume, by some metrics, more whisky than anyone else in the world. A large amount comes from beyond its borders – India is the world’s biggest whisky market by volume, with annual consumption often put at around 1.5 billion litres, though some estimates stretch that to roughly 10 billion bottles when all whisky sold in the country is counted. But the country also produces a vast quantity of its own, although often made in part from molasses, like rum. Not what many would consider to be whisky.
